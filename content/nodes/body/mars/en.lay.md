## What it is {#what}

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun, rocky, with a radius of about 3,390 km — roughly half of Earth's. It has a thin atmosphere mostly of carbon dioxide and two small moons, Phobos and Deimos [@nssdc-mars table "Bulk parameters"].

Inside it has a liquid metallic core with a radius of about 1,830 km, measured by the InSight lander's seismometer from reflected marsquake waves [@stahler-2021 abstract].

## How far away it is {#distance}

Mars orbits the Sun at an average of 228 million km and takes 687 days per orbit [@nssdc-mars table "Orbital parameters"]. From Earth it is 54.6 to 401.4 million km away depending on the planets' positions [@nssdc-mars table "Observational parameters"], so light and radio signals to spacecraft take roughly 3 to 22 minutes.

## Where it is in the sky today {#sky_today .nocite}

Mars's position in tonight's sky — which constellation it is in, when it rises and how bright it is — is computed by the app right on your phone (the sky_core package over Astronomy Engine), with no internet connection.

## What missions found {#missions}

- The Curiosity rover found preserved organic molecules in roughly 3-billion-year-old mudstones in Gale crater [@eigenbrode-2018 abstract].
- The InSight lander measured the size of the core seismically and showed it is larger and less dense than expected [@stahler-2021 abstract].

## Open question: methane {#open_question}

Curiosity measures low levels of methane near the surface in Gale crater that vary with the seasons (Webster et al. 2018) [@webster-2018 abstract]. The ExoMars TGO orbiter, however, found no methane higher in the atmosphere and set an upper limit far below the surface values (Korablev et al. 2019) [@korablev-2019 abstract]. How to reconcile the two results — and whether any source is geological or biological — nobody knows yet [@korablev-2019 conclusion].
