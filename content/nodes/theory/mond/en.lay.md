## Main claims {#claims}

In 1983 Mordehai Milgrom proposed that flat galaxy rotation curves ([[obs.flat-rotation-curves]]) do not require invisible matter; it is enough to assume that at very low accelerations gravity (or inertia) behaves differently from Newton's law [@milgrom-1983 abstract].

The threshold is an acceleration a₀ ≈ 1.2·10⁻¹⁰ m/s², roughly a hundred-billionth of gravity at the Earth's surface. Above it Newton holds; below it gravity weakens more slowly with distance, so orbital speeds at the edge of a galaxy stop falling [@famaey-mcgaugh-2012 §1].

MOND began as a rule for galaxy dynamics only. Relativistic versions that also describe light bending and cosmology came later: TeVeS (Bekenstein 2004) and AeST (Skordis and Złośnik 2021) [@bekenstein-2004 abstract; @skordis-zlosnik-2021 abstract].

## Prediction record {#predictions}

- Milgrom stated in advance that low-surface-brightness galaxies would show the largest mismatch between visible and dynamical mass; McGaugh and de Blok confirmed it in 1998 [@milgrom-1983 abstract; @mcgaugh-deblok-1998 abstract].
- The baryonic Tully-Fisher relation (speed⁴ ∝ baryonic mass) follows directly from the theory and turned out to be tight in the data [@milgrom-1983 abstract; @mcgaugh-2000 abstract].
- Radial acceleration relation: in 153 galaxies the observed acceleration can be predicted from the distribution of visible matter alone, as MOND expects [@mcgaugh-2016 abstract].
- Gaia wide binaries: the result is disputed — Chae reports a MOND signal, Banik et al. find agreement with Newton [@chae-2023 abstract; @banik-2024 abstract].

## Open problems {#open_problems}

- **Galaxy clusters.** Even with MOND, cluster centres lack roughly a factor of two in mass, so the theory needs some as yet unseen matter there [@sanders-2003 abstract].
- **The Bullet Cluster.** Mass measured by gravitational lensing is separated from the hot gas; Clowe et al. read this as direct evidence for dark matter [@clowe-2006 abstract].
- **The CMB.** Original MOND cannot compute the heights of the acoustic peaks; AeST claims it can, but it is a new theory with extra fields that few groups have tested so far [@skordis-zlosnik-2021 abstract; @famaey-mcgaugh-2012 §7].
- **Wide binaries.** The largest analysis of Gaia DR3 data does not see the deviation MOND expects [@banik-2024 abstract].

## Competitors {#competitors}

The main competitor is [[claim.dark-matter-halos]] within [[theory.lcdm]]: it explains the same observations with added matter instead of a changed law [@ostriker-1974 abstract; @planck-2018-vi abstract].

## What would refute it {#would_refute}

Famaey and McGaugh state that MOND would be refuted by a galaxy whose rotation cannot be explained from its visible matter using a₀, and more generally by any low-acceleration system that behaves in a Newtonian way [@famaey-mcgaugh-2012 §6]. Wide binaries are therefore a direct test, and their outcome is decisive for the theory [@banik-2024 abstract].
