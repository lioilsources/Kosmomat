## Main claims {#claims}

Proponents of the Electric Universe hold that electric currents and magnetic fields in plasma play the leading role in the cosmos and that gravity is overrated on large scales. Plasma, they say, forms filaments, and current filaments link stars and galaxies into a single circuit [@thornhill-talbott-2007 introduction; @scott-2006 introduction].

The best-known claim is the "electric Sun": the Sun is not powered by nuclear fusion in its core but by currents flowing in from the galactic environment, and its visible surface is akin to the glow discharge at an anode. Ralph Juergens formulated the idea in the 1970s in the journal Pensée, devoted to the work of Immanuel Velikovsky [@juergens-1972 introduction; @scott-2006 chapter on the Sun].

The movement draws on real plasma physics — Birkeland currents and the work of Hannes Alfvén — but its conclusions appear in books and at the movement's conferences, not in peer-reviewed journals [@thornhill-talbott-2007 chapter on plasma; @juergens-1972 introduction].

## Prediction record {#predictions}

- **Solar neutrinos.** Without fusion in the core, the solar neutrino spectrum should not match the pp chain and the CNO cycle. Borexino measured both, in line with the fusion-powered solar model — see [[pred.eu-sun-no-fusion-neutrinos]] [@scott-2006 chapter on the Sun; @borexino-2020 abstract].
- **Neutron stars and black holes.** Scott rejects them and explains pulsars as electrical phenomena. The GW170817 gravitational waves match the merger of two neutron stars, and the EHT imaged the shadow of the black hole in M87 [@scott-2006 chapter on pulsars and black holes; @abbott-2017-gw170817 abstract; @eht-2019-i abstract].
- **Comets.** Thornhill and Talbott describe comets as charged rocky bodies and count the unexpectedly bright flash at the Deep Impact collision as a success [@thornhill-talbott-2007 chapter on comets].
- The Deep Impact team, however, found water ice and fine dust from a highly porous nucleus in the ejecta; Rosetta measured comet 67P at about 0.53 g/cm³, roughly half the density of water — it cannot be solid rock [@ahearn-2005 abstract; @patzold-2016 abstract].

## Open problems {#open_problems}

- **No quantitative model.** Critics, among them astrophysicist Brian Koberlein, point out that the movement offers no calculations that could be compared with measurements, such as a predicted solar neutrino spectrum or light curve [@koberlein-2014 introduction; @borexino-2018 abstract].
- **Neutral matter.** On large scales matter is electrically neutral, so electric forces cancel while gravity adds up; the theory must explain where the enormous currents it assumes come from and how they are sustained [@thornhill-talbott-2007 chapter on plasma; @planck-2018-vi abstract].
- **The CMB.** The theory has no calculation of its own for the spectrum and acoustic peaks of the cosmic microwave background, which ΛCDM describes to percent-level precision [@planck-2018-vi abstract; @scott-2006 introduction].

## Competitors {#competitors}

It stands against [[theory.lcdm]] and against all of stellar astrophysics based on nuclear fusion; it shares starting points with the peer-reviewed plasma cosmology of Alfvén and Peratt, but not its conclusions [@thornhill-talbott-2007 introduction; @bahcall-1964 abstract].

## What would refute it {#would_refute}

By the theory's own logic, the source of solar energy is decisive: demonstrated fusion in the Sun's core would refute the electric model of the Sun. The pp and CNO neutrino spectrum measured by Borexino is exactly such evidence [@scott-2006 chapter on the Sun; @borexino-2020 abstract].
