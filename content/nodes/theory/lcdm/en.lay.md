## Main claims {#claims}

About 13.8 billion years ago the universe began expanding from a hot, dense state, and it is still expanding. The early universe was a plasma; the [[obs.cmb|cosmic microwave background]] is light released when that plasma cooled enough to become transparent [@planck-2018-vi abstract].

According to the Planck satellite, today's universe is about 5% ordinary (baryonic) matter, about 27% cold [[term.dark-matter|dark matter]] and about 68% [[term.dark-energy|dark energy]], which the model describes with a cosmological constant Λ; the same data give a Hubble constant of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc [@planck-2018-vi Table 2].

P. J. E. Peebles proposed the model in this form in 1984 as a flat universe with cold dark matter and a cosmological constant [@peebles-1984 abstract]. The accelerating expansion that Λ requires was measured by two independent teams using type Ia supernovae in 1998–1999 [@riess-1998 abstract; @perlmutter-1999 abstract].

## Prediction record {#predictions}

- Alpher and Herman predicted relic radiation a few kelvin warm in 1948; Penzias and Wilson detected it in 1965 [@alpher-herman-1948 p. 774; @penzias-wilson-1965 abstract].
- Eisenstein and Hu predicted baryon acoustic oscillations — a preferred separation between galaxies — in 1998; they showed up in SDSS data in 2005 [@eisenstein-hu-1998 abstract; @eisenstein-2005 abstract].
- Light-element abundances: deuterium and helium agree, lithium-7 does not — see open problems [@wagoner-1967 abstract; @fields-2011 abstract].
- Direct detection of dark matter particles has produced no signal so far; the outcome is open [@lz-2023 abstract].

## Open problems {#open_problems}

- **Hubble tension.** Local measurements with Cepheids and supernovae give 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc, about 5σ higher than the value the CMB implies within ΛCDM [@riess-2022 abstract; @planck-2018-vi abstract].
- **No dark matter particle.** The most sensitive underground experiments have seen nothing so far [@lz-2023 abstract].
- **Cosmological constant problem.** Estimates of vacuum energy from quantum field theory exceed the observed value of Λ by tens of orders of magnitude, and no one knows why [@weinberg-1989 §1].
- **Lithium.** Old stars contain roughly three to four times less lithium-7 than Big Bang nucleosynthesis predicts with the baryon density from the CMB [@fields-2011 abstract].
- **Origin of inflation.** The model takes its initial conditions from inflation, but Ijjas, Steinhardt and Loeb argue that Planck data disfavour the simplest inflationary models [@ijjas-2013 abstract].
- **Is dark energy changing?** Depending on the data combination, DESI DR2 gives 2.8–4.2σ in favour of time-varying dark energy; that is below the 5σ discovery threshold [@desi-2025-dr2 abstract].

## Competitors {#competitors}

The main competitor on galactic scales is [[theory.mond]], which modifies the law of gravity at low accelerations instead of adding dark matter [@milgrom-1983 abstract; @famaey-mcgaugh-2012 §1]. Outside the peer-reviewed literature stands the [[theory.electric-universe]] [@scott-2006 introduction].

## What would refute it {#would_refute}

The model would need fundamental change if time-varying dark energy were confirmed above 5σ, or if the Hubble tension survived after measurement systematics are ruled out — both are being tested by large surveys [@desi-2025-dr2 abstract; @riess-2022 abstract].

It would also be refuted if the acoustic scale failed to appear in the galaxy distribution, or if the cosmic composition derived from the CMB and from supernovae disagreed [@eisenstein-2005 abstract; @planck-2018-vi abstract].
