## What astronomers mean by it {#what}

Dark matter is the name for matter that neither emits nor absorbs light and that we know only through gravity. It is an interpretation of observations, not a directly measured thing [@bertone-hooper-2018 §1].

## Where it came from {#origin}

In 1933 Fritz Zwicky calculated that the galaxies in the Coma cluster move so fast that the cluster would fly apart without far more non-luminous matter [@zwicky-1933 §5]. For about forty years the idea was hardly followed up [@bertone-hooper-2018 §3].

## What it rests on today {#evidence}

- Galaxy rotation curves stay flat far from the centre (Rubin, Ford, Thonnard 1980), see [[obs.flat-rotation-curves]] [@rubin-1980 abstract].
- In the colliding galaxy clusters of the Bullet Cluster, the mass measured by gravitational lensing is offset from the hot gas that holds most of the ordinary matter [@clowe-2006 abstract].
- The cosmic microwave background implies that non-baryonic matter makes up about 26% of the universe's density and ordinary matter about 5% [@planck-2018-vi tab. 2].

## What is missing {#open}

No dark matter particle has been caught directly; for example, the LZ experiment in 2023 found no signal and only narrowed down the possible properties [@lz-2023 abstract]. Some astronomers therefore explore modified dynamics instead of invisible matter — [[theory.mond]] [@milgrom-1983 abstract]. The dark matter interpretation is covered by [[claim.dark-matter-halos]].

Dark matter is not [[term.antimatter|antimatter]]: antimatter is produced and measured in the lab [@alpha-2010 abstract].
