## What astronomers mean by it {#what}

Dark energy is the name for the unknown cause of the accelerating expansion of the universe. The name describes the effect, not a mechanism [@weinberg-1989 §1].

## How it was found {#origin}

In 1998 and 1999 two independent teams measured that distant type Ia supernovae are fainter than a decelerating expansion would allow [@riess-1998 abstract; @perlmutter-1999 abstract]. The simplest interpretation is the cosmological constant Λ, once introduced into the equations by Einstein [@perlmutter-1999 §1].

## How much of it there is {#amount}

In the standard model [[theory.lcdm]], the cosmic microwave background implies that dark energy makes up about 69% of the universe's density [@planck-2018-vi tab. 2]. That number is derived within the model, not a direct measurement of a substance.

## Open questions {#open}

- Quantum field theory estimates a vacuum energy many orders of magnitude larger than the observed value; this is the "cosmological constant problem" [@weinberg-1989 §1].
- DESI survey data from 2025 suggest in some combinations that dark energy changes over time, at 2.8–4.2 [[term.sigma|σ]]; this does not cross the 5σ discovery threshold [@desi-2025-dr2 abstract].

Dark energy is neither [[term.antimatter|antimatter]] nor [[term.dark-matter|dark matter]]: dark matter attracts gravitationally, dark energy accelerates the expansion [@planck-2018-vi §2].
