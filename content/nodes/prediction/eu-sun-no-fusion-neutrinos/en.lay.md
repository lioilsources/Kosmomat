In the "electric Sun" model the Sun draws its energy from currents flowing in from surrounding space, not from nuclear fusion in its core [@scott-2006 chapter on the Sun]. Fusing hydrogen into helium necessarily produces neutrinos with a characteristic energy spectrum; John Bahcall calculated their flux from the Sun as early as 1964 [@bahcall-1964 abstract].

This was not a prediction made ahead of measurement. Raymond Davis had been catching solar neutrinos in the Homestake mine since the 1960s — at roughly a third of the calculated rate [@davis-1968 abstract]. Thirty years later this "deficit" was explained by neutrinos changing type on the way; in 2002 the SNO experiment measured the total flux of all types in agreement with the solar model [@sno-2002 abstract].

The decisive test came with the Borexino detector. It measured neutrinos from the first reaction of the pp chain (2014), all main components of the pp chain (2018) and, in 2020, neutrinos from the CNO cycle, which supplies about 1% of the Sun's energy [@borexino-2014 abstract; @borexino-2018 abstract; @borexino-2020 abstract]. The spectrum therefore matches fusion in the core, which refutes this prediction of the [[theory.electric-universe]] — see [[obs.solar-neutrino-spectrum]] [@borexino-2020 abstract].
