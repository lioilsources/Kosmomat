The claim: flat rotation curves ([[obs.flat-rotation-curves]]) are caused by matter we cannot see — an extended halo surrounding each galaxy and reaching far beyond its luminous disc. In 1974 two teams proposed this independently: Ostriker, Peebles and Yahil in the USA, and Einasto, Kaasik and Saar in Estonia [@ostriker-1974 abstract; @einasto-1974 abstract].

The idea of "missing mass" is older. In 1933 Fritz Zwicky calculated that the galaxies in the Coma cluster move so fast that the cluster would fly apart without additional invisible mass; his argument drew little response for decades [@zwicky-1933 p. 125; @bertone-hooper-2018 §III].

The claim no longer rests on rotation curves alone. In the colliding cluster 1E 0657-558 (the "Bullet Cluster"), most of the mass measured by gravitational lensing sits apart from the hot gas that holds most of the visible matter, at 8σ significance [@clowe-2006 abstract]. The cosmic microwave background implies roughly five times more non-baryonic matter than ordinary matter [@planck-2018-vi Table 2].

The weak spot: no dark matter particle has been detected directly. The LZ experiment found no signal in its first run [@lz-2023 abstract]. For an account that needs no halo, see [[theory.mond]]; for the concept itself, see [[term.dark-matter]] [@milgrom-1983 abstract].
